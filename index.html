    National Sum of Lights grew +13.6% per year between 2015 and 2024. The light-to-population elasticity across ADM2 units is 0.66 (95% CI 0.44–0.88, R²=0.53): light is spread more evenly than population, suggesting broad-based service coverage.

    Who lives without detectable light?

    10,609,930 people — 40.0% of the inhabited population — live in cells that are inhabited but emit no detectable night-time light, against 12,249,262 in 2015. The largest concentrations are listed in the table; they are the natural starting point for a targeted off-grid or grid-extension programme. Depending on the noise floor (0.15–0.5 nW·cm⁻²·sr⁻¹), this figure ranges from 10,641,575 to 10,957,188: that is the indicator's real uncertainty.

    How did the lit footprint change?

    The lit footprint moved from 16,328 to 55,599 km² (+240.5%), with 39,716 km² newly lit and 446 km² losing detectable light. The sprawl ratio of 1.56 indicates a pattern of outward spread rather than densification.

    Is light concentrating or spreading?

    Population-weighted inequality in light per capita across ADM2 units narrowed from 0.348 to 0.258 (Gini). 81% of total inequality sits between ADM1 regions and 19% within them. Initially dimmer units grew faster, indicating convergence.

    Limitations — read before citing

    • Thresholds: noise floor 0.25, urban core 10.0 nW·cm⁻²·sr⁻¹; every 'unlit' figure depends on the first of these.
    • The brightest 1 % of pixels hold 57.0% of the national Sum of Lights.
    • Gas-flare candidates detected: 10. Verify against petroleum infrastructure before interpreting.
    • Night-time lights do not measure electricity access, GDP or population — every such reading is an inference.
    • Activity that emits no light is invisible here: subsistence agriculture, daytime informal trade, indoor activity.
    • No DMSP-OLS year is compared with a VIIRS year; the two are different physical quantities.
